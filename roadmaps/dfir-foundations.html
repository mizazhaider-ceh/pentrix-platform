Roadmap

DFIR: Digital Forensics & Incident Response

From securing evidence to writing the incident report, one sequenced path.

Audience
Beginners who want to investigate real incidents, not just watch others do it
Difficulty
Beginner to Intermediate
Length
14-20 weeks
Steps
8 steps

The sequence

0 of 8 steps

Tick a step when you finish it. Progress is saved in this browser only.

  1. 01
    4 h

    Why this comes next: DFIR starts with discipline: order of volatility, chain of custody, and the collect, examine, analyze, report cycle. Skip this and your analysis is guesswork no manager or court would trust.

    • NIST SP 800-86: Guide to Integrating Forensic Techniques into Incident Responseguide · Beginner · Free↗
    • SANS DFIR PostersCheatsheet · All Levels · Free↗
  2. 02
    10 h

    Why this comes next: Disk images are where most investigations start. NTFS structures, deleted files, and carving are the bread and butter of every forensic case, so this is where your hands-on skill begins.

    • AutopsyTool · Beginner · Free↗
    • The Sleuth Kit (TSK)Tool · Intermediate · Free↗
    • File System Forensic Analysis (Brian Carrier, Addison-Wesley, 2005, 600 pp)Book · Advanced · Paid↗
  3. 03
    10 h

    Why this comes next: Once you can read a disk, learn where Windows hides the story: registry hives, Prefetch, event logs, and shell artifacts. KAPE and Zimmerman's tools turn days of manual parsing into minutes.

    • Eric Zimmerman's EZ ToolsTool · Intermediate · Free↗
    • KAPE (Kroll Artifact Parser & Extractor)Tool · Beginner · Freemium↗
    • 13Cubed (Richard Davis)YouTube Channel · Beginner · Free↗
  4. 04
    10 h

    Why this comes next: Fileless malware and injected code never touch the disk. Volatility on a memory capture finds running processes, hidden code, and network connections that disk analysis cannot see.

    • Volatility 3Tool · Intermediate · Free↗
    • MemLabsCTF Platform · Intermediate · Free↗
    • The Art of Memory Forensicsbook · Intermediate · Paid↗
  5. 05
    10 h

    Why this comes next: Packets and logs show what happened on the wire: C2 beacons, exfiltration, lateral movement. Brad Duncan's pcap exercises teach this on real infection traffic.

    • WiresharkTool · Beginner · Free↗
    • Malware-Traffic-Analysis.net Exercisesinteractive lab · Beginner · Free↗
    • CyberChefTool · Beginner · Free↗
  6. 06
    8 h

    Why this comes next: Responders constantly hit suspicious binaries mid-case. Basic static and behavioral triage tells you what a sample does and whether it matters, before anyone commits to full reverse engineering.

    • TCM Security: Practical Malware Analysis & TriageCourse · Beginner · Paid↗
    • MalwareAnalysisForHedgehogsYouTube Channel · Beginner · Free↗
    • The DFIR ReportNewsletter · Intermediate · Free↗
  7. 07
    6 h

    Why this comes next: Findings only matter if people can act on them. The DFIR Report shows how real analysts write; the template gives you the structure for your first report.

    • The DFIR ReportNewsletter · Intermediate · Free↗
    • Incident Report Template (Antti Kurittu)guide · Beginner · Free↗
  8. 08
    20 h

    Why this comes next: Blue Team Labs and CyberDefenders hand you full cases with evidence and questions to answer. GCFA is the certification that proves you can do the whole job.

    • Blue Team Labs OnlineInteractive Lab · Beginner · Freemium↗
    • CyberDefendersCTF Platform · Intermediate · Freemium↗
    • GIAC Certified Forensic Analyst (GCFA)certification · Advanced · Paid↗
