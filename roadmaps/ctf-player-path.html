Roadmap

CTF Player Path

From your first picoCTF challenge to competing with a team on CTFtime.

Audience
Beginners who want to go from their first flag to competitive team play
Difficulty
Beginner to Advanced
Length
24-36 weeks
Steps
8 steps

The sequence

0 of 8 steps

Tick a step when you finish it. Progress is saved in this browser only.

  1. 01
    10 h

    Why this comes next: picoCTF runs year-round with challenges ordered by difficulty, so you learn the core loop first: read the challenge, poke at it, grab the flag, submit it. That loop is the whole game, and it transfers to every other platform.

    • picoCTFCTF Platform · Beginner · Free↗
    • Root-MeCTF Platform · Beginner · Free↗
  2. 02
    12 h

    Why this comes next: Crypto shows up in every CTF, and it rewards writing code over guessing. CryptoHack teaches encoding, XOR, and modular arithmetic by making you break things, while CyberChef and dCode handle the quick decodings in between.

    • CryptoHackCTF Platform · Beginner · Free↗
    • CyberChefTool · Beginner · Free↗
    • dCodetool · Beginner · Free↗
  3. 03
    15 h

    Why this comes next: Web is the most approachable real category after general skills. Natas walks you through server-side tricks one level at a time, and Hacker101 fills in the attack vocabulary you will reuse on every web challenge.

    • OverTheWire Natasinteractive lab · Beginner · Free↗
    • Hacker101course · Beginner · Free↗
    • PortSwigger Web Security AcademyInteractive Lab · Beginner · Free↗
  4. 04
    10 h

    Why this comes next: Forensics is pattern recognition, and it is where beginners score early points. MemLabs hands you memory dumps with questions attached, and Wireshark plus CyberChef cover the network captures and file carving around them.

    • MemLabsCTF Platform · Intermediate · Free↗
    • WiresharkTool · Beginner · Free↗
    • CyberChefTool · Beginner · Free↗
  5. 05
    15 h

    Why this comes next: Rev turns scary binaries into readable logic. crackmes.one has thousands of tiny challenges sorted by difficulty, and the free Reverse Engineering for Beginners book teaches the assembly you need alongside them, with Ghidra doing the heavy lifting.

    • crackmes.oneCTF Platform · Beginner · Free↗
    • Reverse Engineering for Beginners (Dennis Yurichev)book · Beginner · Free↗
    • GhidraTool · Beginner · Free↗
  6. 06
    20 h

    Why this comes next: Pwn is rev with consequences: once you can read a binary, pwn.college teaches you to exploit it from first principles, and pwntools becomes the Python library you write every exploit with.

    • pwn.collegeInteractive Lab · Beginner · Free↗
    • pwntoolstool · Intermediate · Free↗
    • GEF (GDB Enhanced Features)Tool · Beginner · Free↗
  7. 07
    15 h

    Why this comes next: Modern binaries have stack canaries, ASLR, and NX, so simple overflows stop working. ROP Emporium teaches return-oriented programming one gadget at a time, and Nightmare's documented writeups show the full path from binary to shell.

    • ROP EmporiumCTF Platform · Intermediate · Free↗
    • Nightmare (guyinatuxedo)Course · Beginner · Free↗
    • LiveOverflowYouTube Channel · Beginner · Free↗
  8. 08
    10 h

    Why this comes next: CTFtime is the calendar and scoreboard for the whole scene: pick a beginner-friendly event, play it, then read the winning writeups for every challenge you missed. WeChall tracks your progress across challenge sites, and both platforms are where teams recruit new players.

    • CTFtimeCTF Platform · All Levels · Free↗
    • WeChallcommunity · Intermediate · Free↗
    • HackTheBoxCTF Platform · Intermediate · Paid↗
