# Hunting around exams: how I kept the bounty grind alive during 60/60

First year at Howest, MTS3-CS: 60 out of 60 ECTS, 16.40 GPA, no retakes. And I was hunting bugs the whole time. People ask how, like there is a secret. There is no secret. There is a system, and the system is mostly about what I stopped doing.

This is not my study system post. That one covers how I study. This one covers the negotiation between two full-time obsessions: the degree and the hunt. Because they will fight for your hours, and if you do not manage the fight, the exams win by default and the hunting dies quietly.

## The truth about the tradeoff

Let me be honest first: during exam periods, hunting almost stopped. Not slowed. Stopped. In the two weeks before exams, I touched no targets, wrote no reports, read no disclosures. Zero. And that was the plan.

The mistake is trying to do both at full intensity. You cannot. Exam season needs deep, uninterrupted study blocks. Hunting needs deep, uninterrupted focus blocks. They are the same resource. Spending it twice is how you fail both.

So I run the year in seasons, and each season has one priority.

## The three seasons

**Season one: semester (hunting season).** During the semester, coursework is steady but manageable. This is when I hunt. Two to three sessions a week, usually evenings, ninety minutes each. Not marathons. The goal is consistency: keep the eyes sharp, keep one or two targets warm, keep submitting.

**Season two: exam approach (wind-down).** About three weeks before exams, hunting drops to one light session a week, and only recon-style work: reading responses, mapping targets, taking notes. No deep exploitation attempts. The point is to keep the habit warm without spending the deep-focus budget that now belongs to studying.

**Season three: exams (full stop).** Two weeks, zero hunting. The targets will be there in February. The exam is on one specific date. This is not discipline, it is arithmetic.

Then the cycle resets, and the first hunting session after exams feels like coming home.

## Why hunting made me a better student (and vice versa)

Here is the part nobody expects: the two obsessions feed each other.

Hunting taught me to read precisely. Bug bounty is reading HTTP responses character by character, noticing the one field that does not belong. That exact skill, applied to exam questions, is devastating. I catch the "except," the "which of these is NOT," the trick in the question stem, because my eyes are trained to spot the anomaly. Recon eyes read exam papers better.

Studying taught me systems thinking. University courses force you to learn the boring fundamentals: how networks actually work, how operating systems manage memory, how databases store data. Every one of those fundamentals is a hunting superpower. My information disclosure findings came from understanding what a normal API response looks like, which came from coursework. The degree is not separate from the hunt. It is the hunt's foundation.

And the 60/60 system itself, labs before theory, notes like you teach, weekly rhythm, transferred directly. I approach a new target the way I approach a new course: map the territory first, drill the fundamentals, then go deep. Same brain, different subject.

## The rules that kept both alive

**Rule one: the calendar is the boss.** My week has fixed blocks: study blocks and hunting blocks, written down. Not "I will hunt when I feel like it." Feelings are unreliable. The calendar does not negotiate. During the semester, Tuesday and Thursday evenings are hunting. During exam season, they are not, and I do not feel guilty about it, because the calendar decided, not my motivation.

**Rule two: no context switching inside a block.** When I am studying, Burp is closed. When I am hunting, the textbooks are closed. The fastest way to ruin both is the half-and-half evening where you stare at a target while feeling guilty about the exam and stare at the textbook while thinking about the target. One thing at a time, full attention, then stop.

**Rule three: hunting is the reward, not the escape.** This one took me a while. In my first months, I used hunting to procrastinate on studying: "I will just do one quick recon session" at 11 PM before an exam. That is not hunting. That is avoidance wearing a hoodie. Real hunting happens in its calendar block, with fresh eyes. Everything else is just anxiety with a terminal open.

**Rule four: protect sleep like a scope.** Tired brains do not find bugs and do not pass exams. I have lost count of the nights I almost traded sleep for "one more hour" of either. The bugs found at 2 AM are almost always reported badly and rejected. The studying done at 2 AM is almost always forgotten. Sleep is not the opposite of grinding. It is part of it.

## What I would tell a student hunter

If you are in university and hunting bugs, you do not have a time problem. You have a priority problem, and it is solvable.

Decide your seasons before the semester starts. Write down the hunting blocks and the study blocks. When exam season comes, stop hunting without guilt, because stopping was the plan. When the semester runs, hunt without guilt, because hunting was the plan. Guilt is what happens when you never decided.

And remember what the degree is for. The 60/60 was not the point. The point was building the fundamentals that make me dangerous on a target: networking, systems, the ability to learn anything fast. Every exam I passed was a hunting skill in disguise. Alhamdulillah for both.

The grind is not about doing everything all the time. It is about doing the right thing in its season, fully, and then stopping. Hunt in hunting season. Study in exam season. Sleep in both.

That is the whole system. It fits in a calendar and it got me 60/60 and paid bounties in the same year.
