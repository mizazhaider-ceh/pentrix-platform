# Swag and small wins: why the $80 bounty beat the 500 euros

In March 2026 I earned two bounties for information disclosure: 500 euros and $80. The 500 euro one is the headline. It is the number people ask about. But the $80 bounty is the one I think about, and the 'NO BUGS LEFT BEHIND' swag on my shelf means more to me than either payout. Let me explain, because this is really a post about motivation, and motivation is the actual currency of bug bounty.

## The 500 euros felt like... not much

This is going to sound ungrateful. It is not. Alhamdulillah, 500 euros is real money, and I am genuinely thankful for it. But here is the honest sequence of feelings: the email arrived, I felt a spike of excitement for about ten minutes, I told someone, and then... it was just a number. It went into the account. Life continued.

Big payouts have a weird property: they raise the bar for what feels good. After 500 euros, a part of my brain started whispering that smaller findings were not worth the effort. Why spend six hours for maybe $80 when I once got 500? That whisper is poison. It is how hunters end up chasing only the big game, burning out, and quitting. The big win quietly reprogrammed my reward system to only respond to big wins.

I had to actively fight that. The $80 bounty is how.

## The $80 bounty taught me more

The $80 finding was harder. Not technically harder, hunting-wise harder. It was on a target I had been working for weeks with nothing to show. Every session ended with an empty notebook. I was in that dangerous phase where you start wondering if you have lost it, if the first wins were luck, if you should just focus on university and forget the whole thing.

Then one evening, doing the boring work, reading responses on an endpoint I had already looked at twice, I saw it. A field that should not have been there. I almost scrolled past it. I did not.

The report was clean. It got accepted. $80.

Eighty dollars did not change my life. It changed my month. Because it arrived at the exact moment I needed proof that the process works when the process is all you have. The 500 euros proved I could find a good bug. The $80 proved I could keep going through a dry spell and come out the other side. Those are different lessons, and the second one is the one that keeps you hunting for years.

I keep the $80 screenshot in my wins file. I look at it more than the 500 euro one. On bad days, I do not need to remember that I once got lucky on a big one. I need to remember that grinding through nothing eventually produced something. That is the memory that gets me to open Burp on a tired Tuesday.

## The swag: 'NO BUGS LEFT BEHIND'

And then there is the swag. A package arrived with 'NO BUGS LEFT BEHIND' on it, and I am not embarrassed to say I was more excited about that box than about either payout notification.

Here is why. Money is private. It goes into an account and nobody sees it. Swag is physical. It sits on my shelf. When I am studying at 1 AM for an exam and my eyes drift over, it says: you are a person who finds things. It is a trophy, and trophies do something money does not: they make the identity visible.

Bug bounty is a lonely grind. Nobody watches you read your four hundredth HTTP response. Nobody claps when the report gets accepted. The swag is the one piece of the whole invisible process that exists in the physical world. It is proof you can touch. For a student hunting between lectures, that matters more than I can explain.

## The motivation math nobody does

Let me do the actual math of a hunting career, because the payout headlines lie about it.

Say you hunt seriously for a year. You might submit forty reports. Maybe eight get accepted. Maybe three pay well. The rest are duplicates, N/A, informative, or small. If your motivation runs on payouts, you are running on fumes for eleven months of the year. That is not sustainable. Nobody's dopamine system survives that ratio.

So you need other fuel, and you need to choose it deliberately:

**Process wins.** "I mapped the whole target today." "My recon found three new endpoints." "My report was clean on the first try." These are daily, controllable, and real. I celebrate them like payouts, because they are the actual work.

**Skill wins.** "I understand this bug class now." "My Burp speed doubled." "I read that disclosed report and actually followed the chain." Invisible to everyone, permanent for you.

**Identity wins.** The swag on the shelf. The profile with accepted findings. The wins file. Proof that you are becoming the person, not just collecting the outcomes.

**Community wins.** The hunter who answered your question. The writeup you understood. The junior you helped. This game is lonely by default; connection is fuel.

Money is the scoreboard, not the game. The game is the hunt. If you only enjoy the scoreboard, you will quit during the first long stretch of zeros on it. And there will be one. There is always one.

## What I actually optimize for now

I still want the big bounties. Of course I do. I break things for a living, and getting paid well for it is the dream. But I do not measure my weeks in euros anymore. I measure them in sessions completed, targets mapped, reports shipped, and things learned.

The 500 euros was a great day. The $80 was a great lesson. The swag is a great reminder. And the reminder is what I see every day, sitting on the shelf, while I open Burp for session number four hundred and one.

NO BUGS LEFT BEHIND. Including, on some days, the bug called quitting.

Keep the small wins. Frame the swag. The big payouts will come, and when they do, you will already know they were never the point.
